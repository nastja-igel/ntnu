Shore Control Reliance Lab

How should an AI collision-avoidance advisory be presented so that a remote operator relies on it when it is right and intervenes when it is wrong?

A research prototype for the study of appropriate reliance in shore control centres. The AI is scripted with known accuracy, so every operator decision can be compared against ground truth. Three ways of presenting the same advice are compared.

  1. The AI proposes a manoeuvrefor example “Alter course 30° to starboard”, with or without confidence and evidence.
  2. The operator decides in 45 secondsaccept, or reject and choose one of five alternatives. Doing nothing executes the advice.
  3. The prototype records the checking behaviourwhether sensor data was opened, for how long, and which evidence was clicked.
A · ADVICEThe manoeuvre and the type of encounter. Baseline reliance on bare advice.
B · + CONFIDENCEAdds a confidence percentage. Does a number calibrate trust or lull people into accepting?
C · + EVIDENCEGraded confidence, data basis, predicted CPA, COLREG rule and clickable evidence chips. Explanation as an invitation to check.
