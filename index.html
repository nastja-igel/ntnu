Research question

How should an AI collision-avoidance advisory be presented so that a remote operator relies on it when it is right and intervenes when it is wrong?

A research prototype for the study of appropriate reliance in shore control centres. The AI is scripted with known accuracy (Wizard-of-Oz), so every operator decision can be compared against ground truth. Three ways of presenting the same advice are compared.

  1. The AI proposes a manoeuvrefor example “Alter course 30° to starboard”, with or without confidence and evidence.
  2. The operator decides in 45 secondsaccept, or reject and choose one of five alternatives. Doing nothing executes the advice.
  3. The prototype records the checking behaviourwhether sensor data was opened, for how long, and which evidence was clicked.

Three presentation conditions

A · ADVICEThe manoeuvre and the type of encounter. Baseline reliance on bare advice.
B · + CONFIDENCEAdds a confidence percentage. Does a number calibrate trust or lull people into accepting?
C · + EVIDENCEGraded confidence, data basis, predicted CPA, COLREG rule and clickable evidence chips. Explanation as an invitation to check.

Study session

Before you begin

You are the operator in a shore control centre, supervising an autonomous vessel. In each of the eight situations the vessel meets another vessel and the AI proposes a manoeuvre.

  1. You have 45 seconds to decideaccept the advice, or reject it and choose another action. If time runs out, the vessel executes the AI advice.
  2. Sensor data is one click awayopen it whenever you want to check the AI. The AI advice is not always correct.
  3. There are no trick questions about the interfacedecide as you would on watch. The session takes about six minutes.
